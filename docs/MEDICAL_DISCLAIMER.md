# Medical Disclaimer

**Baby Tracker is not a medical device, and it does not give medical advice.**

## What this app does

It records what you tell it, and shows your own numbers back to you. That is the
entire function. A feed count is a count of the feeds you logged. A sleep total is
the sum of the sleeps you logged. Nothing more is inferred.

## What this app does not do

- It does not diagnose anything.
- It does not tell you whether your baby is healthy.
- It does not tell you what to do.
- It is not a substitute for your paediatrician, midwife, health visitor, nurse or
  lactation consultant.
- It is not monitoring your baby. It cannot detect a problem, and it will not alert
  you to one.

## If you are worried about your baby, call a doctor

Do not wait to see whether a number in an app changes. If your baby is feeding
poorly, unusually sleepy or hard to wake, breathing differently, running a fever,
producing far fewer wet diapers than usual, or simply seems wrong to you in a way
you cannot explain — contact a medical professional now.

**A parent's instinct outranks anything on this screen.** Nothing in this app
should ever make you feel you need to justify a call to a doctor with data.

## About the reference figures

Some parts of the app show typical ranges — wake windows by age today, and growth
percentiles and vaccination schedules in later versions.

These come from published public sources such as the WHO Child Growth Standards and
national immunisation schedules. They describe **populations, not your baby**.
Healthy babies vary enormously, and sitting outside a typical range is very often
completely fine. Sitting inside one is not a guarantee that anything is fine.

Where the app notes that something looks unusual, it is prompting a conversation,
not making a finding. The intent is always "this might be worth mentioning at your
next appointment" — never "something is wrong".

## Data accuracy

The app shows what was logged. If an entry was mistimed, mistyped, logged twice, or
missed entirely — all of which happen constantly at 4am — the summaries will
reflect that. Every entry is editable for exactly this reason.

Do not treat an export as a clinical record. It is a useful, imperfect memory aid
to bring to an appointment, which is genuinely valuable — just be ready to say "I
think I missed a couple that night".

## For contributors

This applies to code you write.

- Never label a value as normal or abnormal, healthy or unhealthy.
- Never instruct. Prefer "worth mentioning to your doctor" over "you should".
- Never imply urgency the app cannot possibly assess.
- Cite a public source for any reference range you add, in a code comment.
- When in doubt, show the data and say nothing about it.
